The Vasa Warship

The Vasa is a 17th-century warship that sank on its maiden voyage in 1628. After being salvaged in 1961, it was restored and is now displayed at the Vasa Museum in Stockholm, Sweden. The ship is notable for being the only preserved vessel of its kind from the era.

Historical Information

The Vasa was commissioned by King Gustavus Adolphus of Sweden and built to showcase Swedish naval power. Despite its grandeur, the ship was top-heavy and sank in the Stockholm harbor within minutes of its launch. The story of the Vasa encapsulates issues of naval engineering and the political ambitions of Sweden during the 17th century.

Eva Fidjeland's Contribution to the Restoration Project

Eva Fidjeland played a critical role in the Vasa restoration project, particularly in the restoration of its stained glass elements. Her expertise in stained glass artistry enhanced the visual narrative of the vessel, drawing connections between historical maritime heritage and contemporary artistry.

Timeline of Key Events

1625: Construction of Vasa begins.

1628: Vasa sinks on its maiden voyage.

1961: Vasa is salvaged from the sea.

1990s: Eva Fidjeland begins her work on the restoration.

2000: Completion of key restoration projects involving stained glass.

The Connection Between Eva Fidjeland's Work and the Vasa Museum

Fidjeland's stained glass works not only adorn the Vasa Museum but also tell stories of the ship's journey and Sweden's maritime history. Her meticulous craftsmanship parallels the dedication shown in the Vasa's original construction and restoration, making her contribution an essential part of the Vasa narrative.
